/ Definition

Sworn, plain or consular

A sworn translation is done by a translator on the register of the Polish Minister of Justice. It carries a seal, a register number and a certification formula – which is why offices, courts, banks and universities accept it. It is needed wherever a document has legal effect: residence card, marriage, transcription of a record, a court case, a loan, recognition of a diploma. The translator describes everything they see: seals, signatures, watermarks, crossings-out – and notes whether the translation was made from the original or from a copy.

A plain translation has no official standing. It is fine for personal use, correspondence, a CV or a website, but no office will accept it. If you are not sure which one you need – send a photo of the document and tell us where it is going. We reply before quoting.

A consular translation is a document prepared to a specific consulate's requirements or certified by a consul – for visa and passport matters, consents for a child to travel and powers of attorney. Requirements differ, so before we start we ask what the consulate expects.

From the original or from a copy? A good scan or photo is enough for a quote and for the translator's work. If the office requires a translation "from the original", you show the original when handing over or collecting documents – the translator compares it with the scan and uses the right formula. Without the original, the translation is marked "from a copy", which some offices accept and some do not.

/ In one order

Translation and Apostille without two firms

A sworn translator does not issue apostilles, and an Apostille agent does not translate. Clients usually discover this at the counter, when the office asks for "Apostille and translation" – and start a second case with a second firm. With us it is one order: we check whether the document needs the certificate, obtain it at the MFA, and the translator translates the document together with the certificate. The order matters.

It works the same way in reverse. A Polish document going abroad: MFA Apostille, translation into the destination country's language, an Apostille on the translator's signature if needed. A document from Ukraine for a Polish office: we obtain the certificate through a partner in Ukraine and translate into Polish in Warsaw – Apostille for documents from Ukraine.

/ FAQ

Frequently asked questions Sworn translations

Will the office accept the translation?

+

Yes. Translations are done by translators on the Minister of Justice's register, with a seal and a register number. They are accepted by the Voivodeship Office, registry offices, the social insurance office, courts, banks and universities across Poland. We prepare them to the specific office's requirements so they do not come back for correction.

How is a page of sworn translation counted?

+

A standard page is 1,125 characters with spaces of the finished translation, counted in the target text, not the original. Every page started counts as a full page. Descriptions of seals, signatures and translator's notes are part of the text too, which is why a certificate with several seals can be longer than it looks. We give the page count before you order.

How long does a sworn translation take?

+

As standard 2–4 working days from payment confirmation; a single certificate is usually faster. Next-working-day express – by arrangement, depending on language and volume. If the document also needs an Apostille, add 1–3 working days for the MFA.

Which languages do you translate?

+

Most often Ukrainian, Russian and English – both ways, from and into Polish. German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and French – through partner sworn translators. Other languages on request, just ask.

Do I have to provide the original document?

+

A legible scan or photo is enough for a quote and for the translation. The original is needed when the office requires a translation "from the original" – then you show it at handover or collection, and the translator uses the right formula. The original is also essential if the document is to receive an Apostille.

Is a translation made abroad valid in Poland?

+

Usually not. Polish offices accept translations made by a sworn translator on the Polish register or certified by a Polish consul. A notarised translation from Ukraine or a "certified" translation from the UK has to be redone in Poland – cheap in the issuing country, but useless at the counter.

Can a sworn translation be electronic?

+

Yes – the translator can issue a PDF signed with a qualified electronic signature. Some offices and most online institutions accept it, but it cannot be apostilled and not every counter takes it. If you are not sure what the recipient accepts – order the paper version; you get the scan right away anyway.

Can an Apostille go on the translation?

+

Yes. The MFA then confirms the sworn translator's signature – needed when a Polish translation is to be used abroad. Condition: the original translation with the translator's handwritten signature and seal, not a scan. Order of steps: Apostille on the document first, then translation, finally Apostille on the translation.

Can I order several documents at once?

+

Yes, and it is the most common case – a set for a residence card or the registry office usually means several documents. From 6 pages in one order we give 10% off, and we file all documents needing an Apostille with the MFA in one go.
