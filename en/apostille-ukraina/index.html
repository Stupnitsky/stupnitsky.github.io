Apostille Ukraine

Apostille for documents from Ukraine – without leaving Poland

We obtain the Apostille for your Ukrainian diploma, school certificate, birth or marriage certificate through a partner on the ground, so you don’t have to travel to Ukraine. You’ll need one if a Polish office, university or employer asks for the document with an Apostille – for a residence card or at the registry office (USC), for example. You send us photos, we handle the case in Kyiv and the document comes back to Warsaw with its Apostille. If you need a sworn translation, we do it here, priced separately from the Apostille. If you don’t have the document to hand, we obtain a duplicate.

Check which scenario is yours

We take on every case. If the document cannot be obtained, we refund the prepayment – you pay only for shipping.

An archive in occupied territory, a university that no longer exists, a certificate left behind in a flat – we try anyway. Nobody knows in advance whether an archive was evacuated, and the only way to find out is to file a request. All you risk is the cost of shipping.

/ The most common order

Diploma, school certificate and the diploma supplement

For recognition (nostrification), university admission or work in your profession, a Polish university or office usually wants the diploma with an Apostille – and almost always the diploma supplement with your grades as well, because it’s a separate document with its own Apostille. The Apostille is issued by the Ukrainian Ministry of Education and Science. You already have the diploma, so there’s nothing to obtain – which is why this is the fastest scenario.

When was your diploma issued? A modern diploma with its supplement takes about two weeks. An old paper diploma with a paper supplement takes longer to process – 1–1.5 months. A Soviet diploma can be done too, without a duplicate, in about a month and a half. It only fails if the university no longer exists, or stayed in occupied territory and its archive wasn’t evacuated – but we try even then, and all you risk is the cost of shipping.

/ Before you write

What cannot be apostilled

An electronic Apostille from Diia

Polish offices accept only a paper Apostille. If you get an electronic one yourself through the app, you end up with a document a Polish office won’t accept, and lose both money and time. The paper Apostille has to be physically brought to Poland, and that’s exactly what we do.

A laminated document

An Apostille can’t be placed on a laminated document. The way round it is a new copy from the registry office, followed by the Apostille – scenario two.

Pre-2016 records and Soviet documents

These aren’t apostilled anywhere, so they always go through a duplicate. The exception is diplomas: old and Soviet diplomas are apostilled without a duplicate, it just takes longer.

A traffic police certificate

This can’t be obtained on someone else’s behalf. We don’t accept these orders, so that we never have to turn you down halfway through.

Obtaining court documents

These can’t be obtained on someone else’s behalf either. But an Apostille on a court decision you already have – yes, we can get that.

Criminal record certificate

Most clients get it themselves in Diia – it’s the only document you can get there entirely online – and come to us just for the sworn translation. We can obtain it with an Apostille on request.

/ Your originals

“What if the document gets lost in transit?”

Documents travel in a single Nova Poshta parcel with a declared value and insurance. You get the tracking number on the day it’s sent and can follow the document both ways. Before sending, we scan every page, and both you and we keep the scan.

We’re working with a country at war, and it would be dishonest to promise zero risk. So here is plainly what happens if the document doesn’t arrive after all: we obtain a duplicate at our own expense and take it through to the Apostille. You don’t pay for it separately, and you don’t pay twice.

The only exception is documents that can’t be duplicated because the archive is in occupied territory. If your document comes from there, tell us straight away and we’ll decide together whether to send the original at all or go for a duplicate from the start.

Would you rather not part with your only original? In Ukraine a notary can make a certified copy, and the Apostille goes on that instead – just mention it when you ask for a quote. Our liability and complaints rules are set out in the Terms of Service (in Polish).

/ Step by step

Photos first, originals later

  1. Photos and a description. Through the quote form, send a photo of the document (if you have it) and of your Ukrainian internal passport, plus your tax ID number and your registered address in Ukraine. Tell us what you need the document for in Poland.
  2. Check and quote. We check the year of issue, the condition of the seal and any lamination, then tell you whether it’s an Apostille straight away or a duplicate first. You get the final price and the timing.
  3. Payment up front. By BLIK, bank transfer or cash. For archive searches in frontline areas, the prepayment is refunded if the archive refuses, less the cost of shipping.
  4. The original. Hand it over at a meeting in central Warsaw or send it via an InPost parcel locker. If there’s no document, skip this step. No power of attorney is needed.
  5. Ukraine and back. The document travels by Nova Poshta to Kyiv, gets its Apostille (or a duplicate first) and comes back to Warsaw. We keep you updated at every stage.
  6. Translation and collection. If you need a sworn translation, we translate the document together with its Apostille, charged separately at 90 zł per page. You collect the full set in person or from a parcel locker, ready to submit to the office.

/ FAQ

Frequently asked questions Documents from Ukraine

Does a Ukrainian document actually need an Apostille in Poland?

+

You’ll find claims online that the 1993 Poland–Ukraine agreement on legal assistance makes the Apostille unnecessary. Some offices do accept a Ukrainian document without one, but voivodeship offices, registry offices (USC) and universities often ask for it, including for a residence card. So check with the office where you’re submitting your documents – and if you do need an Apostille, we’ll get it without you having to travel.

Can I get the Apostille myself through Diia?

+

You can, but Polish offices don’t accept an electronic Apostille – only a paper one. Since February 2026 Ukraine has had a new procedure with an electronic register, a fee of 670 hryvnias and three working days for processing. The difference between those three days and our 2–3 weeks is logistics: the paper document has to be submitted, collected and brought to Poland.

Do I need a power of attorney?

+

No, neither a consular nor a notarial one. To obtain a duplicate, four photos taken on your phone are enough: the old document (if you have it), your internal passport, your tax ID number and your registered address. For comparison, a power of attorney at the consulate costs 125–250 zł and needs an appointment.

My birth certificate was issued before 2016. What now?

+

A certificate like that can’t be apostilled. We obtain a new copy from the Ukrainian registry office (you don’t need to travel or sign a power of attorney) and then get the Apostille on it. It costs 1 100 zł and takes 5–6 weeks in total. It’s not a dead end – just two stages instead of one.

My document is from Donetsk, Mariupol or Kherson. Is it still possible?

+

It’s possible if the archive was moved to territory controlled by Ukraine – and the only way to find out is to file a request. It takes at least a month and a half, with no guarantee. If the archive hasn’t survived, we refund the prepayment and you pay only for shipping. We take on these cases because you risk nothing but the cost of shipping.

Is the translation included in the price?

+

No. The 550 zł covers the Apostille, the official fees in Ukraine and shipping both ways. A sworn translation into Polish is charged separately at 90 zł per page. We do it after the Apostille, because the Apostille has to be translated too. It’s all one order, and you get a complete set ready to submit to the office.

It’s cheaper in Ukraine. Why do you charge 550 zł?

+

Because you’re paying for more than a stamp: we check the document before you pay, keep track of deadlines, send it insured both ways, refund the prepayment if the archive refuses and answer you in English – and you can hand over the original in person in central Warsaw if you’d rather not give it to a courier. We take the case through to a document that’s ready to submit to the office, not just to the Apostille. If you need a sworn translation, we’ll do it as part of the same order, but it’s charged separately at 90 zł per page.

Which documents from Ukraine are needed for a residence card?

+

Most often a birth certificate (yours and your children’s), a marriage certificate, and a diploma or school certificate if you’re coming to study or to work in your profession – with an Apostille and a sworn translation. You can usually get the criminal record certificate yourself in Diia, and we translate it. The office lists the documents it needs in its letter (wezwanie) – attach that list to the quote form and we’ll quote for the whole set.

Can I order from a country other than Poland?

+

Yes, for obtaining the document and the Apostille: the document is prepared in Kyiv and sent by Nova Poshta straight to you, in any EU country. Our sworn translations from Ukrainian into Polish are for Polish offices. If the document is going to an office in another country, ask there what kind of translation they accept.
