Apostille Ukraine

Apostille for documents from Ukraine – without leaving Poland

A diploma, school certificate, birth or marriage certificate issued in Ukraine must receive an Apostille at the Ukrainian ministry before a Polish office accepts it – for a residence card, the registry office, a university or an employer. We handle it through a partner on site: you send photos, we run the case in Kyiv, the finished document with the Apostille comes back to Warsaw, and we do the sworn translation here. If the document is not to hand, we obtain a duplicate.

We take on every case. If the document cannot be obtained, we refund the prepayment – you pay only for shipping.

An archive in occupied territory, a university that no longer exists, a certificate left behind in a flat – we still try. Nobody knows in advance whether an archive was evacuated; the only way to find out is to file the request. All you risk is the cost of shipping.

/ The most common order

Diploma, school certificate and the transcript

For recognition, university admission and work in a regulated profession, a Polish university or office wants the diploma with an Apostille – and almost always the supplement (transcript) with grades too, because it is a separate document with its own certificate. The Apostille is issued by the Ukrainian Ministry of Education and Science. The diploma is in your hands, nothing needs obtaining – which is why this is the fastest scenario.

When was your diploma issued? A modern diploma with a supplement – about two weeks. An old paper diploma with a paper supplement – processed longer, 1–1.5 months. A Soviet diploma – also possible, no duplicate needed, about a month and a half. It fails only when the university no longer exists or remained in occupied territory with its archive not evacuated – but we try even then, and you risk only the shipping cost.

  • Diploma + transcriptPLN 1,100

/ Before you write

What cannot be apostilled

An electronic Apostille from "Diia"

Not accepted in Poland – paper only. Anyone who gets the Apostille themselves through the app ends up with a document a Polish office will not accept, losing money and time. The paper certificate has to be physically brought to Poland – that is exactly what we do.

A laminated document

No Apostille on lamination. The way out: a new copy from the civil registry, then the Apostille – scenario two.

Pre-2016 and Soviet documents

They are not apostilled anywhere. Always via a duplicate. Exception: diplomas – old and Soviet ones are apostilled without a duplicate, just slower.

A police (former traffic police) certificate

Cannot be obtained on someone else's behalf. We do not take such orders, so as not to refuse after the fact.

Obtaining court documents

Also impossible on someone else's behalf. An Apostille on a ruling you already hold – yes, we do that.

Criminal record certificate

Most clients get it themselves in "Diia" – the only document that goes fully online there. They come to us just for the sworn translation. Obtaining it with an Apostille – on request.

/ Your originals

"What if the document gets lost in transit?"

Documents travel in a single Nova Poshta shipment with declared value and insurance. You get the tracking number on the day of dispatch and can follow the document both ways. Before shipping we scan every page – the scan stays with you and with us.

We work with a country at war, and promising zero risk would be dishonest. So we say plainly what happens if the document does not arrive after all: at our own expense we obtain a duplicate and take it through to the Apostille. You pay for that neither separately nor twice.

The only exception is documents with no source for a duplicate: archives in occupied territory. If your document comes from there, tell us straight away – we will decide together whether to send the original at all or go the duplicate route from the start.

Prefer not to part with your only original? In Ukraine a notary can make a certified copy and the Apostille goes on that – mention it when asking for a quote. Liability and complaint rules are set out in the Regulamin (in Polish).

/ Step by step

Photos first, originals later

  1. Photos and a description. Send a photo of the document (if you have it), your Ukrainian internal passport, your tax ID number and your registered address in Ukraine. Tell us what the document is for in Poland.
  2. Check and quote. We check the year of issue, the seal, lamination – and tell you whether it is an Apostille straight away or a duplicate first. We name the final price and the timing.
  3. Payment up front. Bank transfer or BLIK. For archive cases from frontline areas – the prepayment is refundable if refused, less the shipping cost.
  4. The original. Hand it over at a meeting in central Warsaw or send it by parcel locker. If there is no document – skip this step. No power of attorney needed.
  5. Ukraine and back. The document travels by Nova Poshta to Kyiv, gets the Apostille (or the duplicate first) and returns to Warsaw. We report every stage.
  6. Translation and collection. A sworn translator translates the document together with the certificate. You collect the set in person or by parcel locker – ready to file at the office.

/ FAQ

Frequently asked questions Documents from Ukraine

Does a Ukrainian document even need an Apostille in Poland?

+

Online you will read that the 1993 Poland–Ukraine treaty on legal assistance waives the Apostille. Formally true – in practice voivodeship offices, registry offices and universities demand it, including for a residence card, and without the certificate the document comes back. Not worth testing on yourself: a refusal costs more than the Apostille.

Can I get the Apostille myself through "Diia"?

+

You can, but Polish offices do not accept an electronic Apostille – paper only. Since February 2026 Ukraine has a new procedure: an electronic register, a fee of 670 hryvnia, three working days at the authority. The gap between those three days and our 2–3 weeks is logistics: the paper document has to be filed, collected and brought to Poland.

Is a power of attorney needed?

+

No – neither consular nor notarial. To obtain a duplicate, four phone photos are enough: the old document (if any), the internal passport, the tax ID number, the registered address. For comparison: at the consulate a power of attorney costs PLN 125–250 and requires an appointment.

My birth certificate is from before 2016. What now?

+

Such a certificate cannot be apostilled. We obtain a new copy from the Ukrainian civil registry (without you travelling and without a power of attorney), then the Apostille on it. PLN 1,100 and 5–6 weeks in total. Not a dead end, just two stages instead of one.

The document is from Donetsk, Mariupol, Kherson. Is it possible?

+

Possible if the archive was moved to territory controlled by Ukraine – and the only way to find out is to file the request. From a month and a half, no guarantee. If the archive did not survive, we refund the prepayment – all you are left with is the shipping cost. We take such cases because you risk nothing but the shipping.

Is the translation included in the price?

+

No. PLN 550 is the Apostille with official fees in Ukraine and shipping both ways. The sworn translation into Polish is charged separately, PLN 90 per page – done after the Apostille, because the certificate must be translated too. All in one order; you get a set ready for the office.

It is cheaper in Ukraine. Why PLN 550 with you?

+

Because we sell a result, not a stamp: checking the document before payment, keeping to deadlines, insured shipping both ways, a refund if the archive refuses, a translation made for a Polish office and a reply in your language – plus handover in person in central Warsaw if you would rather not give your original to a courier. We run the case through to a document the office accepts, not to a certificate.

Which documents from Ukraine are needed for a residence card?

+

Most often a birth certificate (yours and your children's), a marriage certificate, a diploma or school certificate for study or professional residence – with an Apostille and a sworn translation. The criminal record certificate you usually get yourself in "Diia", and we translate it. Send us the list from the office's request – we will tell you what to Apostille and what not.

Can I order from a country other than Poland?

+

Yes, for obtaining the document and the Apostille: the document is produced in Kyiv and goes by Nova Poshta straight to you, to any EU country. Sworn translation we do only for Polish offices – in Germany or Spain you need a local translator.
