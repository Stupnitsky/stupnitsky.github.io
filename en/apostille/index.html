/ The stamp

What an Apostille is and when you need one

An Apostille is a certificate by which an authority of the issuing country confirms that the signature, seal and capacity of the person who signed a document are genuine. It was introduced by the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961; Poland has applied it since 2005. A document with an Apostille is recognised in all convention countries – today more than 120 states, including the whole European Union, the United Kingdom, the United States, Ukraine, Turkey and Israel – with no further legalisation at a consulate.

The certificate does not confirm the content of the document, only its authenticity. That is why an office abroad usually wants both: the document with the Apostille and its sworn translation. In Poland it looks like an A5 sticker or stamp with ten numbered fields and the heading "Apostille (Convention de La Haye du 5 octobre 1961)".

You need an Apostille when a Polish document has to work abroad: marriage or registering a child in another country, work and a residence permit, studies, opening a company or an account, an inheritance or court case, a power of attorney for someone outside Poland.

An Apostille is not enough when the destination country is not a party to the convention – for example Canada before 2024, the UAE, Egypt, Vietnam or China before 2023. Then the document goes through consular legalisation: the MFA first, then that country's consulate in Warsaw. We check this when quoting, before you order anything.

/ Offices

Who puts the Apostille on your document

That depends on the signature on the document, not its content. Most cases end at the MFA, but a document from a notary or a court must first pass through the District Court, and a criminal record certificate or company registry extract has to be obtained in the first place.

/ Documents

Which documents we Apostille

Civil status records

Birth, marriage and death certificates, a certificate of marital status or of capacity to marry. The Apostille goes on a copy issued by the registry office – full or abridged, also the multilingual one. The copy cannot be a photocopy.

Criminal record certificate

From the National Criminal Register, for work and residence abroad. We obtain it on your behalf, have it sworn-translated and complete the Apostille – in one order.

Company documents

Company registry extract (KRS), CEIDG certificate, articles of association, resolutions, commercial powers of attorney, invoices and certificates. For tenders, banks and registering a branch abroad. A company's private documents first need notarial certification.

Notarial documents

Notarial deed, power of attorney, certified signature, declaration, consent for a child to travel. First the District Court certifies the notary's signature, then the MFA adds the Apostille.

Court documents

Divorce judgments, inheritance decisions, copies of rulings. The District Court certifies them, the MFA apostilles them. For a quote we need a photo of every page with a signature and seal.

Sworn translations

An Apostille can also go on the translation itself – it then confirms the signature of a sworn translator on the Minister of Justice's register. Original with the translator's signature only, never a copy. Order translation and Apostille together.

We do not Apostille photocopies, laminated documents or documents without the signature of an authorised person. If you are not sure your document qualifies – send a photo, we reply before you order.

/ Order of steps

Apostille first, then translation

The Apostille is part of the document and has to be translated too. If the translation is made earlier, it has to be supplemented a second time – another fee and a few more days. So the typical route is: the original gets the MFA Apostille, then a sworn translator translates the document together with the certificate, and finally, if the office abroad requires it, an Apostille also goes on the translation itself.

The exception is foreign documents meant to work in Poland – there the order is reversed: Apostille in the issuing country, sworn translation into Polish here. That is how Apostille for documents from Ukraine works: we obtain the certificate through a partner on site and translate in Warsaw; you never leave Poland. We advise on the order when quoting, before anything is ordered.

/ FAQ

Frequently asked questions Apostille

How long does Apostille take in Warsaw?

+

As standard 1–3 working days for documents headed to the MFA. Express – the same day, if the document reaches us by 11:00 and needs no prior translation. Notarial and court documents first need at least two working days at the District Court.

Where in Warsaw is an Apostille issued?

+

At the MFA Legalisation Section at ul. Krucza 38/42 – by online appointment only. Notarial and court documents are first certified by the District Court at al. „Solidarności” 127. We file and collect documents at both places, so you book nothing and do not travel – details on the contact page.

Does the Apostille go on the original or a copy?

+

On the original or on a copy issued by an office – a plain photocopy will not be apostilled. If you do not want to part with your only original, a notary can make a certified copy, and the Apostille then goes on the notary's signature (via the District Court). A photo is enough for a quote; you hand over the original at handover or dispatch.

Is the translation done before or after the Apostille?

+

Apostille first, then translation. The Apostille is part of the document and must be translated too, so a text translated earlier would have to be supplemented a second time. We advise on the order when quoting, before anything is ordered.

What does an Apostille look like?

+

A stamp or sticker headed "Apostille (Convention de La Haye du 5 octobre 1961)" with ten fields: country, who signed the document, in what capacity, with which seal, place and date of certification, authority, number, seal and signature of the official. In Poland the MFA places it on the last page of the document or on an attached sheet. Every Apostille can be checked by number in the MFA register.

Does an Apostille expire?

+

The certificate itself does not expire. The document may: offices abroad often require a civil status record or criminal record certificate no older than 3–6 months. So it is better to get the Apostille when the document is actually needed, not "in advance".

When is legalisation needed instead of an Apostille?

+

When the destination country is not a party to the Hague Convention – among others the UAE, Egypt, Vietnam, Thailand. Then the MFA issues a legalisation instead of an Apostille, and the document must also be confirmed by that country's consulate in Warsaw, usually for an extra consular fee. We handle this route too – when quoting we check which one applies.

Can I go to the MFA myself?

+

Yes – with an appointment booked in the MFA system, a printed confirmation, the PLN 60 fee and collection in person. Free slots are often days away, and a document from a notary must pass through the court first. Our price is the same fee plus the work: checking the document, both visits, the order of steps and handover or shipping.

Is an Apostille needed within the European Union?

+

Not always. Since 2019 EU Regulation 2016/1191 exempts some public documents from the Apostille between member states – mainly civil status records and criminal record certificates when presented to a public authority (not a bank or employer). In practice many institutions still ask for it. Ask the recipient before ordering – and we will tell you whether a multilingual standard form will do instead of an Apostille.
